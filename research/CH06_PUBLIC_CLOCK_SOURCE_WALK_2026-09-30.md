# Chapter 6 — Public ownership and the protection clock

Primary passages checked September 30, 2026: [HM Treasury's final NatWest account](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/government-completes-exit-from-natwest--2), May 30, 2025, sale history and closing table; [NAO's Asset Protection Scheme report](https://www.nao.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2010/12/1011567.pdf), December 2010, internal pp. 6–7, paragraphs 9–13. Both were accessible live. Held text copies are in `research/EDITORIAL_PUBLIC_BORDER_SOURCES_2026-09-29/` as `natwest.md` and `aps_nao.txt`. The complete stored-loss correction note and previous public-border editorial report were read alongside the chapter.

The Treasury chronology gives the 84.4-percent peak public stake, sales beginning in 2015, ordinary dividend receipts from 2018, and the final 2025 exit. The chapter translates ownership into a contingent recovery route: dividends and a sale price, rather than a demand for repayment of the purchase price. The town-employer comparison explains equity exposure; the public powers and network effects of banking exceed the household analogy. The £34.98 billion final receipt total includes five categories of proceeds, with separate fees and special dividend-right payments.

The NAO's first-loss discussion ties the £60 billion threshold to expected losses. Paragraph 12 specifies a two-year payout delay for recovery offsets and repayment of public payouts on early exit. These terms make the existing risk-allocation scene a sequence through time. The 2010 report describes conditional obligations; the existing scheme-close source separately establishes no payouts before the 2012 exit. The chapter does not turn a contractual possibility into an executed public payment.

The existing RBS impairments, capital supplied, guarantee exposure, final public cash shortfall, and avoided damage remain distinct quantities. No measured stored-loss series or rescue-specific inflation estimate is introduced. The expansion makes the signed terms and recovery dates visible while retaining the chapter's strong conclusion: the rescue's architecture selects who can be kept whole and who carries unresolved risk.
