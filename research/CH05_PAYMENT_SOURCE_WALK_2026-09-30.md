## The till had to be made

Exhibit 1 reports $125,000 recovered from a 2014 hopper truck, $15,000 from a skid steer, and $7,500 from propane forklifts. The parenthesized numbers preceding the first equipment entries are list identifiers; the chapter does not infer quantities of machines from them. Receivables over ninety days contributed $116,659.45. Other listed receipts include collected receivables, tax refunds, and named recovery actions. The $1,658,613.82 total is recovered gross receipts, not total historic enterprise value. This scene gives the administration defense physical substance without asserting that every fee was necessary or efficient.

## A class pool becomes individual checks

On June 7, 2024, check 30008 paid the Teamsters Local 559 health and insurance plan $59,780.44, leaving $50,142.69 in the consolidation account. Check 30009 paid Green Mountain Feeds $333.39, leaving $49,809.30. The $50,142.69 corresponds to the general unsecured category's total distribution. Subsequent general unsecured rows show the rounded 30.92-percent dividend, until the account reaches zero. The register's chronological order executes a distribution; it does not create the legal priorities. Earlier estate payments occurred in other accounts, so this sequence is not the complete history of estate administration.

Green Mountain's $1,078.35 was asserted and allowed in full. Its $744.96 shortfall is between allowance and distribution, rather than an inferred failure at the recognition gate. The report gives no date for that creditor's initial proof of claim. The case's 100 months of pendency cannot be called its measured individual wait.

## One claim, a changed recipient

Check 30010 recorded a June 7 payment of $1,257.67 to PC Tech LLC on claim 3. The register marks it voided on October 21. It records the reversal as a negative disbursement, restoring the amount to the account, and check 30026 paying $1,257.67 to Andrew Shawn Donaldson after an October 17 transfer of claim, document 495. The final claims exhibit names Donaldson. These are two positive check entries and a reversal for one claim distribution, not two independent recoveries. The chapter's explanation demonstrates why a final snapshot and a gross sum of positive transactions each lose part of the story.

The trustee certified full distribution of funds and submission of bank statements, deposit slips, and canceled checks to the United States Trustee. We have the filed certification and register; this pass did not inspect those underlying bank materials. The final report supports payment evidence at the trustee-account level. It does not establish the private purchase price of the transferred claim or either party's investment return.

The existing LoPucki and Moody's comparisons retain their separate units and samples. This pass adds no causal estimate of ranking, no measured gate-conversion rate, and no inferred motive for differences between asserted and allowed class totals.
