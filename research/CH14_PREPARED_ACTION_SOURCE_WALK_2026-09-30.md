# Chapter 14 — A prepared occasion reaches action

Read the chapter, its prior agency source walk, the agency operational protocol and the held primary-check summary. Reopened the full Gollwitzer and Brandstätter 1997 primary article, especially Study 2, pages 191–192, on September 30, 2026.

Primary: https://sparq.stanford.edu/sites/g/files/sbiybj19021/files/media/file/gollwitzer_brandstatter_1997_-_implementation_intentions_effective_goal_pursuit.pdf

The experiment assigned 86 volunteer Munich students to conditions, balanced by gender, one week before Christmas. Both groups were instructed to write during the two holidays after Christmas Eve. Planning included a selected time/place, visualization, silent commitment and recording the choices. All received a report form and addressed stamped envelope.

39 reports returned; three lacked identifying codes. Among the 36 identifiable returns, 12/17 planners and 6/19 controls wrote within the requested two days. This denominator is not all 86 randomized participants. The paper reports no significant condition difference in total mailed reports. Mean self-reported writing dates were 2.3 versus 7.7 days after Christmas Eve; postmarks 4.9 versus 12.6. The writing-to-mailing gap did not significantly differ. Postmarks support earlier timing rather than independently observing the actual writing occasion. The paper itself says immediate initiation was not directly observed in this field study.

The new passage follows the action's preparation and tests the rival of flattering self-report. The keys-and-bag image explains advance arrangement; the experiment did not test that image or measure myelination. The chapter retains the wider 94-test meta-analysis, existing conduction/replay units and practical next-action argument. No new experiment was run. The practical inference is that an earlier decision can give a later occasion a recognizable action, without claiming the study measured the book's whole seven-day routine.
