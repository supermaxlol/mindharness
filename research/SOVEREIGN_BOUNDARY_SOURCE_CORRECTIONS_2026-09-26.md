# Sovereign-boundary corrections for Book V3 Chapter 7

Date: 2026-09-26. The source prose is `paper_book/chapters/ch07_sovereign.tex` and `bookv2/chapters/ch07_sovereign.tex`; the new narrative chapter is `bookv3/chapters/07_the_border_that_moved.md`. This note preserves the earlier mechanism while identifying what its cited cases actually establish.

## Primary case chain

1. [Deutsche Bank v. Sri Lanka, ICSID ARB/09/2, award](https://icsid.worldbank.org/sites/default/files/parties_publications/C8394/Claimants%27%20documents/CL%20-%20Exhibits/CL-0142.pdf), ¶¶27–50, 477–491, 591: 2008 oil hedge; citizens' fundamental-rights petitions; Supreme Court interim payment suspension; Central Bank investigation and direction; majority jurisdiction and treaty breach; $60,368,993 compensation plus interest and $7,995,127.36 legal costs. [Khan dissent](https://www.italaw.com/sites/default/files/case-documents/italaw1273.pdf) disputes that the hedge fits the treaty's investment definition. [ICSID case page](https://icsid.worldbank.org/cases/case-database/case-detail?CaseNo=ARB%2F09%2F2) records the annulment application and discontinuance; this is not a merits affirmance and does not prove payment.
2. [Abaclat v. Argentina, ICSID ARB/07/5, case page](https://icsid.worldbank.org/cases/case-database/case-detail?CaseNo=ARB%2F07%2F5&tab=DOC) records the 2011 jurisdiction decision and 2016 consent award. The [consent award itself](https://icsidfiles.worldbank.org/icsid/ICSIDBLOBS/OnlineAwards/C95/DC9712_En.pdf), section II, states that claimants **received payment** under the agreement and that the settlement admitted neither jurisdiction nor liability. [UNCTAD 2016 case review](https://unctad.org/system/files/official-document/diaepcb2017d1_en.pdf), note 5, reports **$1.35 billion** and more than 60,000 claimants. Earlier V1/V2 rounded to ~$1.4B; use $1.35B when giving the sourced figure.
3. [ICSID recognition/enforcement explanation](https://icsid.worldbank.org/procedures/arbitration/convention/recognition-enforcement): awards are binding and recognizable like final judgments, but actual execution follows national law and sovereign-immunity limits. An award is not a receipt.
4. [South Africa regulatory-chill case study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8422681/): 35 interviews with 36 stakeholders; limited investment-treaty anticipatory chill in nutrition and alcohol, no precedential chill in those areas, a reported delay in tobacco plain packaging while Australia cases were pending, and stronger trade-agreement than BIT effects in the studied sectors. A citation count for Tienhaara or Van Harten is not a measurement of how much regulation was deterred.

## Corrections to the old chapter's causal story

| Old inference | Case record and V3 decision |
|---|---|
| An offshore apex with `A=0 ⇒ K=0` generally makes all domestic recourse impossible. | An asset/collectability cap applies only to a specified defendant, legal claim, and reachable pool. The two treaty cases are **about additional investor standing and forum**, not proof of an assetless trust, a zero cap, or a general offshore extraction chain. Chapter 7 does not claim otherwise. |
| Treaty standing drives domestic citizens' gate `g4→0` and renders the domestic observer powerless. | In the Sri Lanka case, citizens **did** invoke domestic fundamental-rights jurisdiction and obtained a Supreme Court interim order. The treaty provided a separate route for a qualifying German investor to claim against the state. Different rights, claimants, and remedies cannot be reduced to a single passage probability. The book now tests each route through its own filings and result. |
| Deutsche Bank converted CPC's debt into a paid sovereign claim. | The majority ordered Sri Lanka to pay under the treaty after finding specific state misconduct. The public award and ICSID case page do not establish ultimate cash payment. The award included interest and nearly $8M legal costs, which the older summary omitted. The annulment case was discontinued; no committee affirmed the award on the merits. |
| The oil-hedge ruling means every state attempt to investigate or regulate investors is wrongful. | The majority found lack of notice/hearing in the Supreme Court order and specific problems with the Central Bank investigation and stop-payment direction. A real due-process objection survived. Khan's dissent opposed treaty jurisdiction at the investment-definition gate. The V3 story gives each argument its strongest form. |
| Abaclat's ~$1.4B settlement is a liability finding. | The tribunal's final record says **payment had been received**, but the settlement expressly denied any admission of ICSID jurisdiction or international liability. Use it to show access, bargaining, and cash, not a merits precedent that Argentina violated the treaty. |
| ISDS chill is universal and has three consistently measured forms. | The South Africa study found a mixed, sector-specific pattern, with a null on precedential chill and limited BIT anticipatory chill in nutrition/alcohol. The measurable claim is that cross-border dispute risk **can** affect policy timing under particular conditions, not that every unpassed regulation was chilled. |
| Gilens–Page U.S. policy coefficients prove the same treaty gate for domestic citizens. | That paper studies U.S. policy preference associations, not investor-state procedure or Sri Lankan citizens' legal standing. It may be used in a separate political-influence discussion with Bashir's critique, but it is not evidence for a `g4→0` treaty gate and is omitted from V3 Chapter 7. |
| Treaty reform cannot change power because every new gate is captured. | States can revise, replace, or terminate treaties, though survival clauses may protect existing investments for years. See [UNCTAD reform options](https://investmentpolicy.unctad.org/blogs/43/time-for-action-concrete-steps-for-iia-reforms) and [termination note](https://investmentpolicy.unctad.org/publications/40/messages). Political choices are consequential, and their timing and vested-right costs are part of the analysis. |
| A treaty award automatically creates taxpayer inflation. | Chapter 6 already separated fiscal and monetary channels. A treaty award first creates a state obligation; who funds it and with what macroeconomic effect requires further budget and monetary evidence. Neither Deutsche nor Abaclat establishes an inflation path. |

The *barzakh* image from Qur'an 55:19–20 remains a useful boundary metaphor and is identified as interpretation. It is not evidence of any treaty's effect. Korea's corporate-control reform cycle and the chat-only Rothschild/Sassoon illustrations do not belong in this chapter's evidentiary chain.
