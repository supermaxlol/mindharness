## The two papers of April 23, 2002

The operating subsidiaries incurred debt. Part went to bonuses for more than fifty managers and executives; the rest moved through HCC to KB Holdings, where it financed repurchase of 65 percent of the top company's stock from the Bain defendants and insiders. The court's approximately **$120 million** describes distributions to the sponsor, affiliates, managers, and others. The later study's **$85 million** Bain payout and calculated **370 percent** return come from published reporting and have a narrower recipient set. Neither number should be silently substituted for the other.

On the same day, Robert Feldman wrote to Big Lots' general counsel and certified that KB Holdings would have consolidated net worth of at least **$20 million** after the repurchase. The court says no contract required the certification and Big Lots had no contractual right to block the deal. Big Lots alleged that the letter was intended to induce its cooperation. In rejecting the pleaded fraud theory, the court found no facts showing a legal right Big Lots surrendered in reliance on it. The letter is therefore a concrete test of the chapter's distinction between **a record a creditor receives** and **a right that lets the creditor intervene before payout**. The court did not decide that the $20 million certificate was false.

KB Toys petitioned for Chapter 11 on **January 14, 2004**, after surviving the 2002 holiday season and 2003. Big Lots' $45 million pay-in-kind note had accrued to about **$57 million**. The court dismissed Big Lots' individual suit, chiefly because the pleaded corporate injury belonged to the bankruptcy estate for creditors collectively. It did not decide that the recap caused the bankruptcy. The later working paper's incidental “2012” date for this case is contradicted by the dated court record.

## The comparison that tests selection

The revised paper identifies **1,572** recaps among **61,628** U.S. buyouts, 2.6 percent of deals and 9.3 percent of buyout volume. Recap targets are generally larger and healthier. The raw ten-year distress rates are **9.2 percent** for recap targets and **3.4 percent** for control firms, with distress defined as bankruptcy or restructuring. The raw gap cannot by itself separate leverage from selection.

The authors use sponsor relationships with banks underwriting CLOs in the loan-purchasing phase to isolate credit supply for recap loans. They compare firms in similar industries, backed by similarly sized sponsors, with buyouts at similar times; they report a strong first stage, no anticipatory dip in the dynamic first stage, and robustness using older bank relationships and bank entry into CLO underwriting. The exclusion assumption is that the relationship-bank CLO opening does not change the firm's distress except through the induced recap. The paper's **22.4 percentage point** ten-year IV estimate is a local effect for credit-induced recaps; the authors explicitly note that the coefficient scales a small change in recap probability to a full treatment switch. Chapter 3 says “22 percentage points” and locates it in that induced population. The **84 percent** typical debt increase is a separate descriptive measure.

The study finds higher deal IRR without a statistically clear mean increase in total value multiple; fund-level causal estimates show lower cash multiples and lower public-market-equivalent returns, with no significant fund IRR effect. The older-loan price result is an observed association, separate from the principal instrumented result. These distinctions allow the chapter to show a return-clock incentive without claiming that all recap deals fail or that every creditor-price movement is a causal estimate.

**Metaphor boundary:** The bakery is a one-owner illustration of a time shift. A buyout has operating subsidiaries, holding companies, lenders, managers, a sponsor, fund investors, and older claimants. Chapter 3 follows the dates and decision rights; Chapter 4 follows the legal address of the note. Bank of America's January 2010 consolidation is a separate institution and accounting event, not the later consequence of KB Toys' recap.
