# Two Memories + Save: Fast Labile, Slow Structural, and Sleep as the Save Button

*Why you must learn twice — once to hold it, once to keep it.*

---

You have two memories, not one. Fast holds what just happened for minutes to hours. Slow holds what usually happens for months to years. Sleep is the save operation that converts one into the other. Skip it and most of what looked learned today is gone by tomorrow.

### 1. Fast vs Slow: Two Clocks

**Fast is labile, cheap, detailed, fragile. Slow is structural, expensive, schematic, durable.** Fast forms in seconds; slow in hours to days.

**Fast = hippocampus:** Early-phase LTP forms in **~1 second** after a 100 Hz burst, boosts EPSP amplitude **+40–60%**, and decays in **1–3 hours** without follow-up. It reuses existing proteins and phosphorylates AMPA receptors already in the membrane — no new mRNA. You can lay down **thousands of traces per day** and lose most by morning.

**Slow = cortex:** Late-phase LTP needs **3–4 spaced bursts** or dopamine-gated novelty, triggers new protein synthesis in **30–90 minutes**, and reorganizes spines over **6–24 hours**. It lasts **months to years**. One concept uses **10⁴–10⁵ cortical neurons** with each synapse nudged only **1–5%**.

RAM vs SSD: RAM writes in **1 second**, holds **1–3 hours**. SSD writes in **hours**, holds **years**. You buffer in RAM and choose what deserves disk.

### 2. Tag-and-Capture: The Budget Rule

Paying for protein after every event would bankrupt the brain. Tag-and-capture (Frey & Morris 1997) splits marker from material.

**Tag (seconds):** A strongly active synapse sets a local tag — CaMKII, actin remodeling — lasting **60–120 minutes**. It marks "something happened here."

**PRPs (minutes–hours):** A strong, novel, or rewarded event elsewhere triggers diffuse synthesis of plasticity-related proteins (Arc, BDNF, PKMζ) shared across **50–100 μm** of dendrite for **~1–2 hours**.

**Capture:** Only tagged synapses capture PRPs. Tagged + PRPs → early becomes late. Tagged without PRPs → decays. PRPs without tags → wasted.

Numbers: weak tetanus alone decays in **~2 hours**. The same weak tetanus paired with a strong tetanus nearby **within 60 minutes** lasts **>8 hours**. Tag half-life is **~90 minutes**; beyond that window pairing fails. You hear a name once (tag) — gone by lunch alone, but paired with surprise within the hour it captures PRPs and persists. Salience gates whether PRPs are made at all.

### 3. Sleep as Save: SHY ×<1 and Ripples 10–20×

Daytime is net potentiation. Without correction, firing rates climb **~2×** over sustained wake. Sleep runs two saves.

**SHY — downscaling by ×<1 (Tononi & Cirelli):** During slow-wave sleep (0.5–4 Hz) mean synaptic strength is multiplied by **× < 1.0**, typically **0.80–0.95** per night. Proportional: 1.5 → ~1.30, 1.0 → ~0.85. Ratio kept, absolute down. Evoked slopes drop **10–20%** overnight; AMPA content drops **~15–18%**; spine size shrinks **~10%** in mouse cortex. Total weight ends at **~0.85×** pre-sleep, so tomorrow has room. Without this tax, everything looks equally important.

**Ripples — replay at 10–20×:** Hippocampal sharp-wave ripples (**150–250 Hz**, **50–100 ms**, **~1 per second** in non-REM) replay waking sequences **compressed 10–20×**. A 5-second run through six place fields at **~8 Hz** theta replays in **~250 ms** — same order, **~20× faster**, coupled to spindles (**12–15 Hz**) and slow oscillations (**0.8 Hz**). About **15–20%** of waking sequences reappear, biased **3–5×** toward rewarded routes. Silencing ripples cuts later memory **30–40%** while leaving learning intact.

SHY is defrag (make room). Ripples are file copy (rehearse what mattered, dozens of bursts per sequence per night).

### 4. Hippocampus vs Cortex

**Hippocampus-fast (episode):** Meet three people in 20 minutes — A at minute 2, B at minute 7, C at minute 14. At minute 30 you recall who-said-what at **80–90%**. At 24 hours without sleep: **30–50%**, bindings swapped. You know you saw someone; you misremember which detail went with whom.

**Cortex-slow (schema):** "Inverted yield curves predict recession" tweaks a semantic net by **1–3%** per encounter. One exposure: **5–10%** chance you produce it unaided. **Five spaced exposures over 14 days** with retrieval and sleep: **65–80%** production, surviving **30–90 days**. Fast gives recognition today; slow gives use in June.

Handoff: hippocampus replays the episode at **10–20×** in ripples; cortex at **0.01×** per event extracts regularity over dozens of replays. Block ripples and episodes persist hours but schemas never form.

### 5. When the Save Fails: Build 11 and Drift

**Build 11: 68.6% failure with fast alone.** Our 12-hour probe retested traces taught without a save — no spacing, no sleep replay, no tag→PRP window. At minute 10, **~78%** looked learned (above 0.60). At hour 12, **68.6%** of those early successes fell below threshold. Survivors (**31.4%**) happened to fall inside a PRP window (reward within ~60 min) or were rehearsed. Early score overestimates retention by **~2×** without save. Minute-10 is not memory; hour-12-with-save is.

**Drift: 30% per 12 hours.** Without an anchor, fast bindings corrupt at **~30% per 12 hours** — color, order, source swap — even when old/new detection stays **~70%**. Cortex drifts **~1–2% per 12 hours** toward the schema. Together they yield confident wrong-source recall.

Fast costs **1×** per trace. Slow costs **10–20×**. Tag-and-capture is the budget rule: pay slow only for tagged.

### 6. How to Study

**Tag well:** One focused pass, 5–20 minutes per concept, with generation — write the idea in 10 words before re-checking. Recall-then-check tags **2–3×** stronger at 24 hours than re-reading.

**Capture in window:** **2–3 retrieval attempts spaced 10–20 minutes apart** after first pass. Each success supplies PRPs. Aim for **70–80%** success — hard enough to need effort, easy enough to succeed.

**Slice by sleep:** Same-day score is a fast score. First sleep **within 12 hours** of learning; deep sleep early in the night carries most downscaling and ripples. **90 minutes** of non-REM after learning rescues **~20–30%** vs equal wake. All-night wake loses **~30%** of bound detail.

**Multiply by <1 deliberately:** Three 20-minute sessions on day 1, 3, 7 beats one 60-minute session by **40–60%** at day 30. Interleave types (A-B-A-B) — small ×<1 between reps that improves transfer **15–25%**.

**Test the save:** Probe after sleep, with new examples, for production not recognition. Recognition after one pass is **~75%** (inflated). Production after sleep + spaced retrieval is **~55–65%** — lower today, truer tomorrow.

### 7. Connection to Gradient + 5

The gradient says **1 crisp photon in at 5,800 K → ~20 dilute photons out at 255 K**, same energy, 20× disorder, with **0.15** you must hold to cycle again. Five organs each answer one way that gradient kills you.

| Gradient demand | Organ | Solves | Without it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Whose loan? | **Skin** | Keeps inside vs outside | Leakage |
| **Spend now or keep?** | **Two memories** | **Fast vs slow; sleep saves** | **30%/12 drift or rigidity** |
| Spend on what? | **Spotlight** | Allocates scarce 0.85 | Noise drowns signal |
| When to stop? | **Brake** (SHY ×<1) | Caps runaway potentiation | Saturation |
| Did it earn? | **Watcher** (γ) | Did saved trace change next act? | Perfect record, zero steer |

Two memories is the temporal budget. Fast spends now — act next minute. Slow keeps — act next month. Sleep reconciles them: ripples at **10–20×** spend fast replays to buy slow structure; SHY at **×<1** taxes everything so tomorrow has room. Remove fast: **68.6%** of apparent learning was never tagged. Remove slow: **30% per 12 hours** lost. Remove sleep: no **10–20×** rehearsal, no **×<1** room — fast saturates, slow never forms.

```
World → Skin (whose) → Two memories (when to keep) → Spotlight (what) → Brake (×<1) → Watcher → World
```

*Fast tells you what just happened. Slow tells you what usually happens. Sleep decides which "just" becomes "usually" — replaying it 10–20× faster while quietly taxing everything by ×<1 so tomorrow has room to learn again.*

## 1. The Functional Model We Have Built
We propose a human as an embodied, continuously learning, predictive, self-monitoring system that must stay itself while changing while entropy rises: WORLD/ENV → PERCEPTION → INTERNAL MODEL (Memory + Prediction + Context) → VALUATION (reward/threat/goals) → ATTENTION → THOUGHT → SELF-MODEL → [Automatic → impulse] vs [Reflective → awareness of impulse] → DELIBERATION ("What do I endorse?") → INTENTION/CHOICE → ACTION → OUTCOME → LEARNING → Updated Memory/Habits/Values/Predictions/Self-model → LOOP

System is recursive: not merely models world, but models itself modeling world. Self = constructed control model integrating memories, bodily states, goals, relationships, predictions, future selves → narrative I am this kind of person. No-self ≠ nothing — processes exist, entity doesn't. Map still guides. Thoughts arise nonconsciously (NONCONSCIOUS → thought/urge → AWARENESS → feed/don't feed → elaboration or fade); impulse is event not instruction (I want to keep scrolling → I notice I want → Do I endorse? → No → act differently — basis of agency). Awareness makes information available; attention decides what receives processing; both distinct from consciousness and from memory.

Consciousness as control process: selected information becomes available for flexible control — attention, planning, comparison, future simulation, self-monitoring. Simple automatic stimulus → response vs flexible situation → represent alternatives → remember past → simulate future → compare → choose — huge advantage for novel situations.

Free will as compatibilist, functional: increasing ability to act according to endorsed values even when another system pulls elsewhere. Not escaping causality, but organism becoming cause: impulse → awareness → alternatives → values/consequences → deliberation → endorsed intention → action → feedback. Parachute example: automatic jumping = danger + conscious context parachute/training/controlled → different expected outcome → recontextualized prediction. Repeated choice → plasticity → stronger pattern → more automatic — conscious choice trains automatic system, second-order loop.

Remaining question H1: Is subjective experience simply what it is like for such integrated self-monitoring system to operate, or could system perform all functions — perceive, integrate, remember, plan, self-monitor, select, report, learn, modify — without experiencing anything (functional zombie)? User intuition: no; genuine awareness of own processing is experience; no inner observer required (homunculus rejected: no little observer, system models itself).

Awareness vs attention vs metacognition vs self-consciousness: Awareness (information available), attention (what receives further processing), metacognition (representing own states with confidence), self-consciousness (I am experiencing this).
