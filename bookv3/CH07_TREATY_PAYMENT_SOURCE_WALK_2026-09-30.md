## The mass claim needs a maintained record

Procedural-history entries v, x–z, and ee record the October 1, 2012 claimant database and annexes, February 8, 2013 confirmation of the expert and team, August 31 final verification report, and August 11, 2014 revised list of verified claimants. The chapter uses these events to show the work between admitting a mass claim and maintaining the people and rights represented by it. The closing record does not supply every verification outcome, and this pass makes no claim about how many holders were rejected, retained, or paid from the initial population.

## The closing chronology

Section II records an agreement in principle on January 31, 2016 and settlement agreement on April 21. On October 7 the parties asked the tribunal to record the settlement in award form. The document states that the agreement had been fulfilled and claimants had received payment. Section III records the tribunal's decision on December 15; dispatch was December 29. The chapter therefore places payment before the final consent award, rather than turning it into an award-induced damages payment. The document preserves the parties' express refusal to admit ICSID jurisdiction, international liability, or wrongdoing.

The $1.35 billion amount remains separately sourced to the already cited UNCTAD review; the consent record confirms fulfillment without replacing that source's unit. Deutsche Bank's surviving award against Sri Lanka remains a different case, with final cash payment unestablished in the examined archive. The added chronology does not claim that treaty proceedings were the sole cause of Argentina's settlement. It shows the documented route in which the parties negotiated and closed this dispute.

The chapter's interpretive link is explicit in its prose: a mass claim's database becomes an external memory of the people and rights it represents. This is an analytical comparison, grounded in the reported procedure, rather than a new legal rule.
