# Why the consciousness context earns one chapter

**Decision:** Yes. The supplied `consciousness_self_free_will_context.md` now contains 107 numbered core sections plus an appendix and appended transfer material, not reader-facing chapter prose. Its distinct contribution to Book 3 is the question left open by the machine-ledger sequence: **Can a system check a record of itself without there being anyone who experiences the checking?** That question earns Chapter 12, “Is Anyone Home?,” between the three machine chapters and the cross-domain synthesis. The rest of the packet supplies source material for the already drafted agency and practice chapters.

## The chapter's single job

Separate two claims that the word **witness** can blur:

1. A **functional witness** accesses the consequential record and can make a correction change the next decision. This is testable with true, shuffled, and absent records.
2. A **phenomenal subject** is someone for whom a state is experienced. Self-report, persistent memory, and a self-model do not alone decide this question.

This distinction protects the book's main argument. Humans may be harmed by an agent's uncorrected decision regardless of whether that agent experiences anything. If a future agent does experience, it could itself become someone whose interests and losses belong in the ledger. The chapter raises that moral possibility without declaring a result the evidence has not settled.

## Story and elimination

The September 28 rewrite opens with Owen’s command-imagery patient, then Bodien’s larger cohort. The silent-radio and closed-door anchors separate response from experience. Alterable body ownership and Mary’s Room lead into the author’s positive process account, the rival theory tests, and the machine report. A constructed payment decision brings responsibility back to the ledger’s power. The strongest rival shortcuts receive a fair trial:

- **“It says I, so someone is home.”** This confuses a report with what it reports.
- **“It is software, so nobody could be home.”** This assumes a substrate boundary without explaining it.
- **“We cannot settle consciousness, so we cannot govern agents.”** This delays a testable control problem while people can already bear costs.

The 2025 adversarial comparison of Global Neuronal Workspace and Integrated Information Theory is the failed walk: even a preregistered contest involving 256 people and three measurement methods did not select one complete theory. Its relevance is the discipline of the test and the open state of the field; it is **not** an AI-consciousness experiment.

## What stays elsewhere

- The packet's self-model, thoughts, endorsed values, Adlerian direction, and free-will sections are developed in **Chapter 14**.
- The two-minute routine, environmental design, and dated self-record belong in **Chapter 15**.
- Buddhist no-self, Ghazalian psychology, and occasionalism remain available as interpretive and theological source material. They can illuminate the personal chapters without being relabeled as neuroscience or made the subject of this hinge chapter.
- A full survey of consciousness theories belongs in notes or a separate book. The Ledger Principle needs one clear boundary: **a checkable ledger is not automatically a conscious reader of that ledger.**
