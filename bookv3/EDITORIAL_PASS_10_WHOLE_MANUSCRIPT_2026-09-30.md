## The argument held across the book

The Prologue and Act I establish that a maintained record can outlive its maker and alter a later choice. Act II shows legal and accounting records changing the timing, address, priority, and bearer of a financial claim. Act III shows a story changing a reader's available interpretations, then tests whether machine records can be inherited, checked, and corrected. The synthesis asks who may amend an authoritative entry. The coda gives that question to the reader; the outlook makes prospective institutional bets. None of these turns requires the substrates to be identical. The recurring causal question is what record entered the next decision, who could challenge it, and who paid.

The handoffs preserve this sequence. An exhaustive normalized-paragraph comparison found no exact repeated paragraph of sixteen words or more. Repeated terms such as *witness* and *keeper* acquire a new burden in each act rather than restarting the same explanation. The main scope risk now is compression, especially in the human foundation: the opening three sections carry only 5,569 main-text words before finance begins. The finance sequence is 11,599 words, 25.7 percent of the 45,056-word main text. Act III's six chapters are 16,947 words; the two-chapter coda is 6,712 and the outlook 4,229.
