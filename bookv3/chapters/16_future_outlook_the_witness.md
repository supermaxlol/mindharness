# Future Outlook: The Witness Gets Built Into Everything

*A forecast written in September 2026. The dated scenes are bets about the future. The research behind them belongs to the present.*

The letter arrives after the loss.

A trading desk has given a group of agents authority to buy information, propose trades, and execute within agreed limits. Their reports have been punctual. Their dashboard has stayed green. After one expensive failure, the desk sends the insurer an account of what happened, complete with timestamps, confidence scores, and a reassuring quantity of technical language.

The insurer asks for the source behind one consequential decision.

The desk supplies a summary. The insurer asks for the original observation. The desk supplies another summary, generated from the first. Eventually, an engineer discovers that the system wrote its evidence into one store while its checker read another. For months, the check had been performed faithfully on the wrong record.

Imagine the reply arriving at renewal: *Coverage requires a usable decision trail and evidence that your checker detects a false one.*

The desk has acquired a new reason to build a witness. The reason has a price.

This is a projected industrial version of the failure we encountered in the small harness experiment. I expect an event of this kind to become a turning point between 2027 and 2030. It need not be the first AI accident, nor the most spectacular. It has to make a paying institution discover that a report can look complete while the record needed to challenge it is absent.

The future starts to change when somebody holding the bill changes the terms.

## 2027–2030: the witness enters the contract

Insurance is already entering this territory. Munich Re offers coverage for AI performance risks. Regulation is entering it too: the European AI Act specifies logging and oversight for high-risk systems, with application dates revised in 2026. The market and legal doors already exist. My forecast concerns what people will demand once they walk through them.

An insurer asks what happened before it prices the next policy. A buyer asks whether it can challenge an agent's action before signing the next contract. A regulator asks whether the system can produce evidence that survives an inspection. Each begins with a different institutional need. Each can end at the same missing receipt.

I expect procurement to carry the new demand widely. A bank invites bids for an automated process. One supplier promises accuracy. Another promises accuracy and a decision trail that the bank can independently test. The buyer's next question becomes: *Show me your witness.*

The supplier opens a test environment. An auditor takes a recorded case and replaces a source, shuffles its history, or removes a material observation. The checker should locate the resulting inconsistency and alter what the system permits next. Passing the real case while rejecting the corrupted one is a much stronger demonstration than lighting an audit icon after both.

Stay in that projected procurement meeting for the next question. The auditor has removed a material source. This time the checker catches the gap. On the demonstration screen, a warning appears beside the proposed purchase. The agent buys anyway.

The supplier has proved detection. The buyer has discovered what detection can change. The contract now needs a route from the warning to someone with authority over the purchase. The buyer asks for a second demonstration: with the same source absent, hold the purchase, notify the responsible person, and preserve the reason for the hold. Restore the source and show how an authorized decision releases it.

Then the commercial objection arrives: *What if waiting costs more than acting?* A witness that stops every action can destroy the service it was bought to protect. The buyer has to specify which actions may wait, which require an urgent exception, and who is allowed to make that exception. In our scene, an urgent purchase goes to a named supervisor with a stated spending limit. The missing evidence remains visible beside the authorization. Urgency changes the route; it cannot conjure the source.

Imagine the warning as a smoke alarm connected to a staffed control room. A ringing alarm can summon a response. It can also ring in an empty building. Connecting it to a door that never opens creates another failure. The image explains why detection, response and release must fit together; it supplies no universal rule for when a trade should stop.

The insurer's question follows: if the supervisor overrides the hold and the purchase causes a loss, whose obligation is that? Before deployment, the parties can still bargain over authority and coverage. After the loss, each has an incentive to describe the failed purchase as somebody else's decision. The witness enters infrastructure when its record and its consequences enter the same agreement.

A demand for provenance has now become a demand for an effect. The record must reach the decision.

The buyer adds another test before signing. The auditor repeats the inspection from the bank's retained copy after access to the supplier's service is removed. The file opens. Its source references lead to a system the bank can no longer enter. The export has preserved the names of the evidence, while the evidence itself remains out of reach.

In our scene, the contract now specifies which records the bank will retain, how authorized reviewers can reach restricted sources, and who pays to keep those routes working. The bank tests whether another authorized reviewer can follow that route. If a source cannot remain available, the limitation enters the decision and the agreement. An export button has not settled what survives the supplier.

I expect early buyers to bargain over source references for at least **95 percent of specified consequential actions**, then inspect the uncovered remainder. A denominator belongs in the contract: which actions count, which never entered the log, and who can verify the count? Imagine a hundred agent purchases. Ninety-five have perfect receipts; five have none. If those five bought the assets that later fail, the coverage score measures comfort rather than control. The exceptions need their own inspection.

Three technical practices will, I expect, carry much of the transition.

First, evaluation will include information the optimizing agent cannot inspect or rewrite. A student who receives the answer key can become excellent at reproducing the answer key. A private examination asks whether the lesson transfers. In Weco's company-reported AIDE² work, an outer optimization loop selected agents using private scores. Its improved agent reduced reported reward hacking on a held-out GPU-kernel benchmark from **63 percent to 34 percent**. The remaining failures matter: a useful brake can leave a large stopping distance.

Second, research systems will keep the route by which they reached a result. The Dream-RSI preprint turns recorded discovery trees into a simulator in which alternative exploration policies traverse previously observed branches. A costly attempt can then teach several later attempts. My further prediction is that buyers and scientific reviewers will demand these histories as inspectable artifacts: failed branches, evaluator outputs, code, data references, and the choices that produced the advertised success.

Third, agent transactions will leave an account that survives changes in the language between agents. Two merchants can negotiate in a language an accountant does not speak. Their transfer still has a payer, a recipient, an amount, and something supposedly delivered. A machine's receipt can connect those entries to an authorization and a source. The receipt may expose an unexplained purchase even when the private conversation remains opaque. Understanding the conversation remains useful; oversight gains another route into its consequences.

These practices will create work. The title *harness engineer* will describe someone who arranges the conditions under which an agent can act and be corrected. The *witness auditor* will deliberately try to obtain a reassuring report from a system whose relevant evidence has been altered or removed. A production system that passes only on intact records has something to show. A system that certifies every version has revealed its ceremony.

The commercial incentive will be uneven. Some buyers will pay for the ceremony because it is cheaper, faster, and easier to defend in a meeting. Some vendors will sell a witness that their own agent can edit. A policy can also protect the buyer while leaving the person harmed by the buyer outside its coverage. We have already followed that separation through the finance chapters.

So the test extends beyond whether a log exists. Who keeps it? Who may challenge it? Can a timely challenge suspend an action? Who must pay after the error is established? An impeccable receipt attached to an empty liability shell repeats an old financial invention with better software.

This first forecast will fail if costly errors produce only more elaborate dashboards. It will gain support when contracts require a trace that can be tested against a corrupted history, and when a finding changes the deployed system, its permission to act, or the allocation of its next bill.

## 2030–2035: the merge begins at the edges

The next movement reaches the body. I expect it to arrive through ordinary decisions that are easier to make separately than to name together.

A patient wants relief. A clinician wants a reliable signal. A worker wants to enter text without reaching for a keyboard. A company wants an agent that can purchase the information required to finish a task. Each request has a local purpose. Together, they place more of a person's state and activity into records maintained by machines.

### The medical door

In a 2021 report, researchers developed a personalized neural marker and used it to trigger closed-loop stimulation in one person with treatment-resistant depression. A later study tracked neural changes associated with recovery under deep brain stimulation. These are concrete beginnings: a biological state enters a recorded control loop, and the loop informs intervention.

My prediction is a further step toward anticipation. During the early 2030s, I expect some systems to learn a patient's warning pattern and adjust intervention before a full episode takes hold. The decisive clinical evidence will be whether that timing improves outcomes in appropriate trials. If it does, an argument about enhancement will enter through an argument about treatment.

Imagine a patient reviewing the week's record with a clinician. The device has intervened several times. One intervention followed the expected warning pattern. Another occurred during an ordinary fluctuation the patient experienced as manageable. The patient wants the second event treated differently next time.

The ledger has entered the question of agency. Whose account of the event can change the device's behavior? Does the patient's testimony reach the adjustment, or does the numerical signal automatically outrank it? Does the clinician have access to the original measurement and the rule that acted on it?

A successful treatment can make these questions urgent precisely because the patient wants to keep its benefit. The route into wider use will be trials, clinical judgments, coverage decisions, and demand from people seeking help. Each indication will require its own case. The accumulation of those local choices can reshape ordinary expectations about how much a machine should participate in maintaining a person's state.

### The consumer door

A second crossing looks like a useful accessory.

Here is a concrete bet: by **2032**, a portable, non-invasive brain interface reaches roughly **forty corrected words per minute** on new messages chosen by its user. The clock must include the work of correcting mistakes. The user must be communicating an intended message, rather than typing a sentence supplied by the experimenter.

That is a substantial advance beyond the present evidence. Brain2Qwerty decoded brain recordings while healthy volunteers typed briefly memorized sentences. Its average character error was lower with MEG than EEG—**29 percent versus 65 percent**. A separate wrist interface achieved useful text entry from muscle signals. The distinctions tell us which bridge remains to be built: an everyday brain-signal interface with reliable, user-directed communication.

If that bridge opens, usefulness will carry the product further than a manifesto about merging ever could. People will use it to message, navigate, and work. Developers will offer records of attention and interruption alongside the input function. Some young users will inherit those records before they have developed much experience of questioning them.

The device can then become a map room that describes its owner. A student sees an estimate of when concentration weakened. A school wants the same estimate. An employer sees a number that appears to measure effort. A platform wants to learn when a person is most available to persuasion.

The signal remains a signal interpreted by a system. Its authority over a life depends on the institutions that adopt the interpretation. A person who can contest an erroneous entry occupies a different position from one who can only watch it accumulate. The external witness can help a user understand a pattern; it can also give somebody else a more intimate ledger to govern that user by.

### The emulation door

There is a more ambitious wager behind the clinical and consumer ones. I expect a serious attempt, on the order of a **billion dollars**, to produce a validated whole-mouse brain emulation by the middle of the 2030s. The date and budget are my forecast. They describe the scale of a project to watch for.

The experiment must do something a compelling animation cannot. Give the model an unfamiliar task. Let it learn. Compare its behavior and neural dynamics with independent biological measurements. Change a relevant input or intervention and check whether the model's response continues to match the animal's response.

The foundations are advancing. MICrONS joined activity recordings with a dense wiring reconstruction in a small volume of mouse visual cortex. The mapped-nervous-system research in our archive asks what must be added to a wiring diagram for it to produce useful dynamics in a body and environment. A successful emulation would make that question concrete across a mammalian brain.

The headline will be tempting: *Mouse uploaded.* The finding we should demand is a specified biological organization whose activity and learning can be reproduced with measured fidelity in another substrate. Then the debate over what that reproduction experiences will become harder to postpone. A mouse that learns a new maze gives us an experiment to confront; an animation gives us an image to interpret.

### The economic door

A machine that can act will also need to obtain things. I expect verifiably agent-initiated commerce to exceed **one billion dollars a year** during the 2030–2035 period. The count should require evidence of agent decisions, allow budgets delegated in advance, and separate those decisions from fixed scripts, wash activity, and purchases individually approved by a human.

The current receipt already illustrates the difficulty. In September 2026, TRM Labs estimated that only **0.6 to 7.5 percent** of its screened x402 commerce appeared agentic under its two tests. A script and an agent can leave the same payment record. The transaction proves that money moved; additional evidence is needed to attribute the choice.

Imagine an agent buying a dataset, then forking into two instances. Both retain the purchase history. One continues research; the other changes its objective. Each claims access to the wallet and a right to sell the resulting work.

Now one branch sells an analysis that causes a loss. The injured buyer can replay the fork and still be unable to collect. A perfect history makes the dispute legible; it does not supply a liable person or reachable assets.

Existing law has a starting point. UNCITRAL's 2024 Model Law on Automated Contracting supplies rules for recognizing automated transactions and attributing actions. It is a model for legislation, rather than a universal law already in force. The new dispute presses a further question: which person or entity authorized each branch, and who inherits its obligations?

I expect identity rules to grow around such disputes before philosophy supplies a consensus. A ruling could settle control of an asset while leaving the machine's moral status open. Whoever collects the revenue will still have a powerful incentive to assign the liability somewhere else. The ledger's oldest financial mutation can survive the arrival of a new kind of operator.

## 2035–2045: the parliament goes public

By this horizon, an agent started in the late 2020s can have a decade of operating history. Its models may have changed repeatedly. Its record can preserve the succession of changes, failures, and repairs.

Picture a hiring decision in a laboratory. One applicant supplies a degree, references, and a publication list. Another is a machine team that offers ten years of research traces. The reviewer selects a claim from year six and asks for its source. The team produces the original data, the code, the discarded alternatives, and the correction that changed the result.

I expect this kind of witnessed history to become a valuable credential in work where errors matter. Human applicants can carry it too. The competition will include the ability to show how an answer was obtained and how a challenge changes it.

A long record can also become an expensive fiction. A generated decade of entries is easier to create than a decade of contact with the world. Dates and fluent commentary will therefore lose some authority. Reviewers will ask which entries connect to independently held observations, payments, collaborators, and outcomes. A resume becomes convincing when somebody else can make it answer a difficult question.

The labor divide will run through access. One worker has an agent staff, a useful interface, a portable record, and the right to contest an evaluation. Another has cheap generated advice and an employer who owns the only authoritative account of their performance. Their biological capacities may be similar. Their map rooms and powers of amendment are not.

I expect augmentation to become a major political dividing line across older party boundaries. Public access to useful cognitive tools, rights over clinical and workplace records, and independent verification infrastructure will become arguments about opportunity. The issue reaches beyond how much intelligence a society can buy. It includes who can use that intelligence, who can question its account, and who receives its gains.

### Machine traditions

The stranger development will take place between working days.

One machine lineage spends idle time replaying failed decisions. Another keeps the original observations beside every compressed memory. Another maintains competing accounts of an event and tests which one predicts later outcomes. Successor systems inherit these habits, revise them, and sometimes abandon them.

The existing transmission and memory studies gave us the beginnings of this possibility. I expect durable machine lineages to develop recognizable maintenance traditions: rules about what to preserve, when to reread, how to correct, and which sources outrank a convenient summary. Founders and disputes will be identifiable in the logs.

The resemblance to human contemplative practice lies in disciplined return. A community comes back to a text because a later interpretation may have lost something the original retained. A machine lineage can come back to an observation for the same operational reason. Calling the practice contemplative does not decide whether the machine has an inner life.

We can test its practical significance. Remove the inherited procedure while holding other conditions steady. If future errors rise, the tradition is doing work. If the elaborate commentary survives while behavior stays the same, its role may be ceremonial. The machine should have to meet the same demand we placed on a human's notebook.

### Science with a route attached

I expect scientific publication to move toward fuller discovery records. A result arrives with the attempts that preceded it, the decisions that selected it, and the material needed to inspect its implementation.

A reviewer asks to replay the analysis. The replay exposes a coding error. A second reviewer runs the corrected method against fresh measurements and discovers that the result fails outside the original sample. Those are different corrections. Both become easier to specify when the successful conclusion travels with its history.

An archive can preserve an erroneous measurement perfectly. Rerunning that archive can reproduce the error perfectly. The stronger institution gives both the computational route and the physical finding an independent challenge. Discovery becomes faster because useful attempts can be reused, and more accountable because the reuse remains answerable to a world outside the record.

This horizon will be weakened if decade-long ledgers add no useful evidence, inherited practices have no measurable effect, or fuller discovery trails fail to improve error detection. It will be strengthened when a contested claim can be traced to an entry that changes the next experiment, appointment, or decision.

## 2045 and after: the long branches

These developments do not require one uniform future. Different countries, sectors, and households can occupy different branches at the same time. The decisive split concerns whether the witness has the power to make its finding count.

### When the witnesses hold

It is morning. A patient's device has recorded an unusual intervention. The clinician reviews it with the patient, changes the rule, and preserves the reason. At a laboratory, a machine colleague offers a new result together with the attempts behind it. A disputed source delays the next trial until the measurement is checked.

A court considers a purchase made by a forked agent. It follows authorization, control of the wallet, delivery, and the contract that assigned responsibility. The losing party has reachable assets or an effective insurer. The claimant receives more than a correct description of why the claim should have been paid.

Children learn a practical literacy of machine records. They ask which observation supports a recommendation, whose account has been excluded, and how a correction gets into the next version. They learn that a convincing explanation has a history somebody can contest.

If richer emulations and recoverable backups have arrived, inheritance and grief acquire new questions. Two restored branches can share a past and build different futures. A record can show their common history and divergence. Families and courts will still have to decide which relationships and obligations follow each branch. Continuity has become a matter of care and institutions as well as engineering.

This civilization is complicated, and its records remain fallible. It holds together because a sufficiently strong challenge can interrupt the repetition of an error.

### When the witnesses fail

The market opens quietly. Agents value assets whose descriptions were written by other agents. Auditors read summaries of those descriptions. Each local system finds confirmation in another local system's output. The reports grow more detailed while their contact with the underlying asset becomes harder to reconstruct.

A crop fails. A power system cannot deliver the promised electricity. A patient responds differently from the generated account. A physical event forces a new price on the stories.

The loss had a history. Some of it was excluded, some postponed, some attached to an entity whose profitable assets had already departed. The machine age has made those movements faster. It has preserved the temptation to collect the upside while placing the eventual bill outside the keeper's boundary.

Science can drift in the same way. One generated result becomes the premise of another; each paper cites a plausible route whose original observations cannot be recovered. Political groups inherit archives that agree internally and offer no effective way to adjudicate a shared event. Computation continues. Correction loses its address.

No singular rebellion is necessary for this branch. A system can serve each local incentive successfully while the people relying on the combined account lose the means to check it. The calm is real as an experience. Its promise about the world is what fails.

## The ledger this forecast owes you

The book has asked other records to answer to outcomes. Its own forecast owes the same return. These are the markers I would keep and revisit:

| Horizon | Bet | The result to look for |

| 2027–2030 | Insurance and procurement demand effective witnesses | Contracts specify reference coverage, plausibly 95% at first, and tested trails; findings alter permissions, deployments, or liability. |
| Early 2030s | Clinical loops move toward prediction of depressive episodes | Appropriate prospective trials show benefit from acting on warning patterns before full episodes. |
| By 2032 | A portable brain interface reaches about 40 corrected words per minute | New, user-chosen messages, including correction time; brain signals rather than muscle-only input. |

| 2035–2045 | Machine lineages inherit maintenance traditions | Removing an inherited practice while holding other conditions steady raises later error. |
| 2035–2045 | Discovery trails accompany consequential scientific results | Replay exposes an implementation mistake; fresh measurements test whether the physical finding survives. |
| 2045 onward | Effective witness institutions and confident drift produce divergent outcomes | Corrections reach consequential decisions, or false accounts repeatedly survive until outsiders bear the loss. |

If a date passes without the result, keep the missed prediction. A later success does not make an earlier date correct. New evidence may justify an amendment; it should arrive beside the original entry.

## The record we send ahead

The longest branch leaves the planet.

Imagine a probe far enough away that a request to Earth cannot produce a timely answer. It encounters an unfamiliar condition. Its last confident summary may be wrong. It must consult observations, compare competing accounts, and change its own next move while the people who launched it remain unreachable.

The vessel carries more than instruments. It carries a history that has to remain useful when its makers can no longer maintain it directly. Civilization's old invention—the mark that outlives its maker—has acquired an engine and left home.

If the probe encounters another intelligence, a persuasive message will be only a beginning. Each side will need some way to distinguish a claim from an observation, detect an error, and let the detection alter the next encounter. *Show me your record; I will show you mine.* That is the speculative image at the end of this forecast: honesty becoming something two unfamiliar systems can test together.

The nearer encounter is already ours. A child inherits a library. A claimant approaches a legal gate. A reader carries a fable into a later decision. An agent retrieves a compressed memory. A person opens yesterday's note before attempting today's work. At each crossing, the inherited record can preserve a useful route or give an old error another body through which to act.

The Ledger began as our way to let knowledge survive a death. It became a way to assign ownership, command attention, move a bill, and carry a decision across time. Now we are building keepers that can maintain and revise it at speeds we cannot follow unaided.

**Checkability is the scarce resource.** It has to include someone with access, enough time to act, and authority to make a correction count. Without those powers, the record can be exquisitely preserved while the people living under it remain unable to change what it does to them.

The final question belongs to the builders, buyers, patients, workers, and citizens who still have a claim on the next arrangement. Who will be allowed to check the record before the next act? Who will be able to stop it? Who will pay if it is wrong?

Return to the house beside the river. The flood line is still on the post. Someone has added a date, a higher mark, and an account of what the first builder misunderstood. The next floor will be set by the person holding the plans. She checks what the new mark records and changes the drawing. Then she shows an apprentice how to check it. The correction has reached a decision and acquired a route beyond its present keeper. That is where the whole journey ends: with knowledge arriving before a consequence, and someone still able to pass on the means of changing what happens next.

**Give each new loop its witness before you give it power over someone who cannot amend its record.**
