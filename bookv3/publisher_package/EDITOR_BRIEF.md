## The book's promise

Whoever writes the record decides who pays. The book follows how inherited records acquire force: a learner repeats a technique, a company assigns an obligation, a court permits an explanation, and a machine selects the history its successor will use. The practical question is whether a warranted correction can reach the person or system making the next consequential decision.

The narrative runs from a maintained biological pattern through human inheritance, financial control, stories and machine records, then returns to agency, a seven-day practice and competing institutional futures. The Ledger supplies continuity; people living with its consequences supply the stakes.

## The reading experience to preserve

Readers encounter a familiar object or physical experience, follow a failure, examine the strongest alternative explanation and earn the inference. Technical detail enters after the reader understands what can be lost. The boundary of each analogy matters where it changes the conclusion.

Keep the book's argumentative force. A proposed cut or qualification should identify the actual repetition, factual conflict or missing step it addresses. The source notes and research archive support revision when a factual question arises. The brief asks for developmental judgment; it does not require turning the narrative into a research paper.

## Scene depth

The payout-day letter, Green Mountain's allowed claim and actual check, Hamilton's prosecution, the wrong-ledger test and the unfinished proposal are important scene anchors. Each contains an earlier account, a consequential action and a later outcome. Ask whether the reader can see that sequence before meeting its general lesson.

The opening act is shorter and traverses more examples quickly. If expansion is warranted, identify a documented attempt, failed transfer or correction that readers need to experience. Added length should deepen a consequence. Historical dialogue, sensations and private motives require support; the manuscript's illustrative scenes can supply accessible bridges without being presented as reported events.

## Requested feedback

Please mark the passage where you first lose the thread, where you wanted another step in a scene, and where the explanation continued after you had understood it. Distinguish those responses from disagreement with the argument: both are useful, and they lead to different revisions.

A first report should prioritize the three changes most likely to improve the reading experience, with chapter or passage locations and the intended effect. We would particularly value a judgment on the opening's depth, the density of Chapter 13, and whether the practical coda and forecast deliver an earned ending.
